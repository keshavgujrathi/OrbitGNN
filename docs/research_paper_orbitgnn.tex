\begin{abstract}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Automatic satellite manoeuvre detection is critical for space traffic management,
yet existing methods are validated on synthetic data, ignore orbital physics, or
discard peer-satellite context. We present \orbitgnn, a physics-informed Graph
Neural Network that detects satellite manoeuvres and estimates their
delta-velocity ($\Delta V$) using only publicly available Two-Line Element (TLE)
records. \orbitgnn\ integrates: (1)~$J_2$-corrected Keplerian physics residuals
computed with the \emph{actual} inter-TLE elapsed time; (2)~a Transformer encoder
(ResidualEncoder) for temporal residual modelling; (3)~an orbital-shell-aware
Graph Convolutional Network (NeighborGCN) for peer context; and (4)~a composite
anomaly score combining self-forecast error, peer-forecast error, and Monte Carlo
Dropout uncertainty, z-normalised across the satellite batch. A novel
$\Delta V$ estimator derives manoeuvre magnitude from TLE-derived $|\Delta a|$
via the linearised Gauss equation. Evaluated on the TLE Observation Benchmark
Dataset~\cite{shorten2023} (9~satellites, 2020--2022, 125~manoeuvre events),
\orbitgnn\ achieves ROC-AUC\,$=\,0.587\pm0.010$ (5~seeds),
PR-AUC\,$=\,0.082\pm0.006$, and event detection rate $61.3\%\pm8.6\%$ within
$\pm72$\,h with 0.24 false alarms/day. All improvements over causal baselines
are statistically significant (vs.\ ResidMag: $p{=}0.0084$, Cohen's $d{=}2.16$;
vs.\ RollingZ: $p{<}0.001$, $d{=}7.03$). Bootstrap 95\% CIs are reported
throughout. Code, models, and results are publicly available.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% =============================================================================

\IEEEPARstart{A}{s} of 2024, over 27,000 artificial objects orbit Earth, including
active satellites that regularly perform orbital manoeuvres for station-keeping,
collision avoidance, and mission operations~\cite{vallado2013}. When a manoeuvre
occurs unexpectedly, ground operators must update conjunction screening promptly;
a delay of hours can increase collision risk and contribute to the Kessler cascade.

The primary public data source for satellite orbits is the Two-Line Element (TLE)
set, published by the US Space Surveillance Network. After a manoeuvre, a new TLE
is typically published 6--36\,h later -- an operational lag for time-critical
assessments. \textbf{Can we detect manoeuvres automatically, earlier, from TLE data alone?}

\subsection{Limitations of Prior Art}
Existing methods share five shortcomings:
\textbf{(L1) Synthetic data}~\cite{mukundan2019,nguyen2022}: trained on idealised
simulations that do not reflect real TLE publication cadences.
\textbf{(L2) No physics}: raw TLE elements are treated as plain features,
conflating secular drift with manoeuvre signatures.
\textbf{(L3) No peer context}: same-shell neighbours experiencing identical
perturbations provide diagnostic signal that is ignored.
\textbf{(L4) Fixed $\Delta t$}: all prior art assumes 24\,h propagation;
real inter-TLE gaps vary widely, causing systematic mean-anomaly errors.
\textbf{(L5) No $\Delta V$ estimate}: manoeuvre magnitude in physical units
requires proprietary telemetry in all prior work.

\subsection{Contributions}
\orbitgnn\ addresses all five limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Real-data pipeline with actual inter-TLE $\Delta t$ for each propagation step.
  \item $J_2$-corrected residuals with angular wrapping, eliminating secular drift confusion.
  \item ResidualEncoder: Transformer encoder capturing 8-step temporal context per satellite.
  \item NeighborGCN: orbital-shell-aware graph convolution with no cross-regime edges.
  \item Composite anomaly score with z-normalised self/peer/uncertainty terms.
  \item Novel $\Delta V$ estimator: $\Delta V \approx n|\Delta a|/2$ from TLE residuals, no telemetry.
  \item Rigorous validation: 5-seed study, 4-config ablation, event analysis, t-tests, bootstrap CIs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% =============================================================================

\textbf{Rule-based.} Patera~\cite{patera2008} used hypothesis tests on TLE Bstar
terms. Kelecy and Jah~\cite{kelecy2012} applied admissible-region methods.
Linares and Jah~\cite{linares2014} used Bayesian filtering for single-satellite detection.
None scale to arbitrary catalogues or provide $\Delta V$.

\textbf{ML on TLEs.} Mukundan and Bhopale~\cite{mukundan2019} (SVM/RF, synthetic,
85\% acc) and Stevenson and Mintz~\cite{stevenson2022} (LSTM, proprietary, AUC$\approx$0.70)
lack physics residuals, peer context, and uncertainty quantification.

\textbf{GNNs for space.} Nguyen et al.~\cite{nguyen2022} applied graph attention to
proximity analysis on synthetic data. No prior work applies GNNs to real TLE-based anomaly detection.

\textbf{Architectures.} ResidualEncoder uses the Transformer~\cite{vaswani2017};
NeighborGCN follows Kipf and Welling~\cite{kipf2017}; uncertainty via MC-Dropout~\cite{gal2016}.

\textbf{Benchmark.} Shorten and Abdurahimov~\cite{shorten2023} published the first
real-TLE benchmark with verified manoeuvre timestamps -- the dataset we adopt and,
to our knowledge, the first ML method to be evaluated on it.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{TLE Loading and Grid Snapping}
For each satellite $k\in\{1,\ldots,S\}$, TLE archives are parsed to yield elements
$\mathbf{a}_k(t)=[a,e,i,\Omega,\omega,M]^\top$ at each epoch. TLEs are snapped to a
daily grid of $T$ steps. For each occupied step $t$, the \emph{actual elapsed time}
$\Delta t_{k,t}$ [s] between consecutive TLE epochs is stored. Steps without a TLE
within $\pm48$\,h are masked ($v_{k,t}{=}0$). \textbf{This actual-$\Delta t$ usage
distinguishes our work from all prior art} (L4).

\subsection{$J_2$-Corrected Keplerian Residuals}

\subsubsection{Propagation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:prop} propagates $\mathbf{a}_k(t)$ forward by $\Delta t_{k,t}$
using secular $J_2$ perturbations (constants: $\mu{=}398600.4\,\text{km}^3/\text{s}^2$,
$R_E{=}6378.1\,\text{km}$, $J_2{=}1.083\times10^{-3}$):

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\small
\caption{J$_2$-Corrected Keplerian Propagation}
\label{alg:prop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require $\mathbf{a}(t)$, $\Delta t$ [s]
  \State $n \gets \sqrt{\mu/a^3}$;\ $p \gets a(1-e^2)$
  \State $\dot\Omega \gets -\tfrac{3}{2}n J_2(R_E/p)^2\cos i$
  \State $\dot\omega  \gets  \tfrac{3}{4}n J_2(R_E/p)^2(5\cos^2i-1)$
  \State Propagate: $\hat\Omega\gets\Omega+\dot\Omega\Delta t$;\ $\hat\omega\gets\omega+\dot\omega\Delta t$
  \State $\hat M \gets \text{NewtonRaphson}(M+n\Delta t,\,e)$
  \State \Return $\hat{\mathbf{a}}(t{+}1)=[a,e,i,\hat\Omega,\hat\omega,\hat M]^\top$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Residual and Wrapping}
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol\delta_{k,t} = \mathbf{a}^\text{obs}_{k,t+1} - \hat{\mathbf{a}}_{k,t+1},\quad
  \text{wrap}(\theta) = ((\theta{+}\pi)\bmod 2\pi)-\pi.
  \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
Angular components are wrapped to $(-\pi,\pi]$ to prevent aliasing.

\subsubsection{Per-Satellite Normalisation}
Training-set statistics $\mu_{k,f}$, $\sigma_{k,f}$ are applied:
\begin{equation}
  \hat\delta_{k,t,f} = (\delta_{k,t,f}-\mu_{k,f})/(\sigma_{k,f}+10^{-6}).
  \label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}

Fig.~\ref{fig:residuals} shows the resulting physics residuals for three satellites.

\begin{figure}[t]

  \caption{Physics residuals ($\Delta a$, $\Delta M$, $\Delta\omega$) for Fengyun-2F (GEO),

    Manoeuvres produce sharp spikes in $\Delta a$ and $\Delta M$.}

\subsection{OrbitGNN Architecture}

\subsubsection{ResidualEncoder (Transformer)}
Input: $\mathbf{X}_k\in\mathbb{R}^{W\times 6}$ ($W{=}8$ steps).
Architecture: Linear$(6{\to}64)$ + positional embedding $+$ 2$\times$ Transformer
encoder layers (4 heads, FF dim 128, GELU, dropout 0.2) + forecast head$(64{\to}6)$.
Output: embedding $\mathbf{h}_k\in\mathbb{R}^{64}$ and self-forecast
$\hat{\mathbf{y}}^\text{self}_k\in\mathbb{R}^6$.

\subsubsection{Orbital-Shell-Aware Graph}
Shell assignment:
\begin{equation}
  s(k)=\begin{cases}
    0~(\text{GEO}) & \bar a_k>30000\,\text{km}\\
    1~(\text{SSO}) & 600\le\bar h_k\le1000,\bar i_k>88°\\
    2~(\text{LEO}) & 1000\le\bar h_k\le1700,60°\le\bar i_k\le70°
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:shell}
\end{equation}
Adjacency: $A_{kj}{=}1$ iff $s(k){=}s(j)$ and $j\in\text{kNN}(k,k{=}3)$; else 0.
\textbf{No cross-shell edges}: GEO drag is $10^5\times$ smaller than LEO; mixing
these signals is physically meaningless.

\subsubsection{NeighborGCN}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{H}'=\text{GELU}(\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{W})+\mathbf{H},
  \label{eq:gcn}
\end{equation}
$\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{64\times64}$ learned; peer forecast head maps $\mathbf{H}'$
to $\hat{\mathbf{y}}^\text{peer}_k\in\mathbb{R}^6$.

\subsubsection{Composite Anomaly Score}
After $N{=}15$ MC-Dropout passes for uncertainty $\bar\boldsymbol\sigma_k$:
\begin{equation}
  s_k = z(\|\mathbf{y}_k{-}\hat{\mathbf{y}}^\text{self}_k\|^2)
       + z(\|\mathbf{y}_k{-}\hat{\mathbf{y}}^\text{peer}_k\|^2)
       + z(\|\bar\boldsymbol\sigma_k\|^2),
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
where $z(x_k)=(x_k-\mu_\text{batch})/(\sigma_\text{batch}+\varepsilon)$
across the satellite batch. This \emph{relative} normalisation automatically
accounts for simultaneous global perturbations.

\subsection{$\Delta V$ Estimation}
From the linearised Gauss tangential burn equation for near-circular orbits:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\Delta V \approx \frac{n\,|\Delta a|}{2}}\quad[\text{m/s}],
  \label{eq:dv}
\end{equation}
$n=\sqrt{\mu/a^3}$. No propulsion telemetry required. Accuracy: $\pm50\%$ for
arbitrary burn geometries.

\subsection{Training}
Self-supervised loss (no manoeuvre labels):
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}=\text{MSE}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^\text{self},\mathbf{y}_\text{next})
             +\text{MSE}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}^\text{peer},\mathbf{y}_\text{next}).
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
Chronological split: Train 60\% | Val 20\% | Test 20\%. Adam, lr\,$10^{-3}$,
100~epochs, early stopping (patience 15). No temporal data leakage.

\subsection{Baselines}
\textbf{ResidMag}: threshold on $\|\boldsymbol\delta\|$ (no ML; causal).
\textbf{RollingZ}: rolling z-score of residual norm, 30-day window (causal).
\textbf{IsolationForest}~\cite{liu2008}: fit on full test set (\emph{non-causal} -- upper bound only).

\subsection{Metrics}
ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, F1 (test-split best-F1 threshold), Event Detection Rate (EDR),
false alarm rate, bootstrap 95\% CI ($B{=}2000$), Cohen's $d$.

\subsection{Implementation}
PyTorch 2.x, Python 3.11, CPU. 105,100 parameters. 5 seeds for full model,
3 for ablation. 157/157 unit tests pass. GitHub Actions CI on Py 3.10/3.11/3.12.

\subsection{Discussion of Limitations}
\textbf{Small satellite set} ($N{=}9$): limits GNN effectiveness and statistical power.
\textbf{Physics model}: $J_2$-only; drag, SRP, lunisolar terms unmodelled.
\textbf{$\Delta V$ accuracy}: $\pm50\%$ for non-tangential burns.
\textbf{Sentinel-6A}: 0\% detection due to launch-phase TLE sparsity.
\textbf{Future work}: larger satellite set, richer physics, irregular-time Transformer,
learned graph structure, streaming inference.

% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================

We presented \orbitgnn, the first physics-informed GNN for satellite manoeuvre
detection evaluated on a real TLE benchmark. \orbitgnn\ achieves
ROC-AUC\,$0.587\pm0.010$, detects $61.3\%\pm8.6\%$ of events within $\pm72$\,h,
and is statistically significantly better than all causal baselines
(vs.\ ResidMag: $p{=}0.0084$, $d{=}2.16$; vs.\ RollingZ: $p{<}0.001$, $d{=}7.03$).
The $\Delta V$ estimator ($\Delta V\approx n|\Delta a|/2$) provides manoeuvre
characterisation from public TLE data alone. The system is self-supervised,
causal, lightweight (105k parameters), and fully reproducible.

\section{Ablation Configuration Details}
\label{app:ablation}
\textbf{Physics Only}: residual norm threshold -- no neural network (= ResidMag).
\textbf{+Transformer}: ResidualEncoder only; graph = identity matrix.
\textbf{+GNN}: graph convolution on per-satellite residual means; no Transformer.
\textbf{Full}: ResidualEncoder + NeighborGCN + MC-Dropout.

\section{Bootstrap CI Construction}
\label{app:bootstrap}
Sample $B{=}2000$ bootstrap replicates with replacement from
$(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{y})\in\mathbb{R}^N\times\{0,1\}^N$.
Compute metric $\theta^*_b$ per replicate. 95\% CI:
$[\theta^*_{0.025},\,\theta^*_{0.975}]$ (percentile method).

\section{Kepler Equation Solver}
\label{app:kepler}
Newton-Raphson on $f(E)=E-e\sin E-M$:
$E_{n+1}=E_n-(E_n-e\sin E_n-M)/(1-e\cos E_n)$,
converges in $\le5$ iterations for $e<0.015$.
